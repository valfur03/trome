TROME needs to know which station you are near, not who you are.
In Apple’s sense, TROME collects no data about you: what reaches my server is used to answer, then not kept.
Here is what the TROME iPhone app does with your data, what my server keeps, who gets it, and what your rights are.

## In short

- TROME has no account and shows no ads.
- The app measures nothing of what you do: I only count how many requests my server gets, never who makes them.
- Your position stays on your iPhone: the app sends it to nobody.
- To show the train times, the app sends my server the identifier of the station you are near, but not your position, and nothing that the app adds to identify you.
- My server writes down nowhere which station was asked for, when, or by whom.
  My host, Cloudflare, sees each request while it passes, [as explained below](#what-my-host-and-the-source-of-the-times-see).
- The App Store page says the same: Data Not Collected.
  What Apple gives me, such as the number of downloads, or your data if you test TROME with TestFlight, is explained [below](#what-apple-gives-me).

## Your position

TROME uses your position, on the iPhone, to find the nearest station, and to show or remove the Live Activity when you come near a station or move away from it.
With **Always** access, iOS tells TROME when you come near a station, even when the app is closed.

The app sends your position to nobody: not to me, and not to anyone else.
You can change this access at any time in **Settings > Apps > TROME > Location**.
Without your position, TROME cannot find the nearest station.

## What the app sends to my server

To show the next trains, the app asks my server for the times.
It sends the identifier of the stop (for example the one of Châtelet station) and a code that every copy of the app shares, which says nothing about you.
It sends neither your position, nor an identifier of your iPhone, nor anything that the app adds to identify you.
The stop that the app asks for shows which station you are near at that moment.

Like any internet connection, the request arrives with the IP address of your iPhone and technical details, such as the versions of the app and of iOS.
My server does not read them and does not keep them.

## Why I use this data, and on what legal basis

I use the identifier of the stop, and the IP address that comes with it, for one reason: to give you the times that you ask for.
Under the GDPR, using data for the length of one request already counts as processing, even if nothing is kept.
My legal basis, the reason that the law accepts, is my legitimate interest (GDPR, article 6(1)(f)): TROME cannot work without this data, the processing is very short, and it keeps nothing that identifies you.
The totals of my server have no stop and no IP address, and I keep them for two other reasons: to watch the daily limit of calls to the source of the times, and to see whether anyone uses TROME.
Their basis is the same: my legitimate interest.
For your messages, the basis is the same: to answer you and to fix TROME.
For TestFlight data and for the numbers that Apple gives me, it is the same: to fix TROME and to see whether anyone uses it.

## What my server keeps, and for how long

After it answers, my server writes down no stop that was asked for: not in its totals, not in its errors, not in its logs.
It keeps only:

- **totals**, for about 30 days: how many requests it gets each hour and each day, how many calls it makes to the source of the times, and how many errors.
  These totals have no stop and no person.
  I keep them for two reasons: to watch the daily limit of calls to the source of the times, and to see whether anyone uses TROME;
- **a list of errors**, for about 7 days: the time, the kind of error and its code.
  It has no stop either;
- **the latest answer of the source of the times for each stop**, as the source gave it, to give it to everyone who asks.
  It stays in the memory of my server, never written down, and the server erases it after about 10 minutes at most.
  It does not say who asked for the stop;
- **the stop for which the source just failed**, with the time of the failure, in the same memory, for a few minutes at most: the server does not call the source again at once.

My server keeps no IP address, no identifier of you or of your iPhone, and no position.
No request log is turned on at my host.

## What my host and the source of the times see

My server runs on Cloudflare.
Cloudflare gets each request before my server does, so it sees the address of the request, with the identifier of the stop, and the network details that come with any web request, such as your IP address and the approximate place that it finds from it.
I turned off every log that Cloudflare could keep for me, and my server keeps none of it.
Cloudflare handles this request for me, as my processor, under its [data processing addendum](https://www.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-customer-dpa/) and its [privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).
Cloudflare is a US company, so it may handle a request outside the European Union, in particular in the United States.
To protect your data there, it relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and on the standard contractual clauses of the European Commission, which its addendum includes.

My server asks Île-de-France Mobilités (PRIM) for the times with the identifier of the stop and the key of TROME only.
PRIM gets nothing that comes from you.

## What stays on your iPhone

TROME keeps your settings (the automatic mode, the snooze and the pauses), the last station shown, and the latest times it got, on your iPhone.
The app sends them nowhere, but they are part of the backups of your iPhone, like the data of every app.
Deleting the app deletes them.

## Where the notifications come from

In this version, TROME makes its notifications on your iPhone, when you come near a station.
I send none from a server, and the app asks for no notification identifier.

## What Apple gives me

For TROME on the App Store, Apple tells me how many times TROME was shown, how many times its page was opened and how many times the app was downloaded, but never by whom.

If you share analytics with app developers (**Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements > Share With App Developers**), Apple gives me crash reports and usage statistics, with nothing that says who you are.
Apple explains it in [Share analytics, diagnostics, and usage information with Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108971).
These usage statistics come only from people who share: the others do not count in them.

If you test TROME with TestFlight, Apple gives me your crash reports, usage data, and the feedback that you send.
If you were invited by email, they come with your name and email address, but not if you joined the test through a public link.
Apple explains it in [TestFlight & Privacy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/test-flight/).

I use this information only to fix TROME, to improve it and to see whether anyone uses it, and I share it with nobody.

## See, correct or delete what I have about you

My server keeps nothing that could lead back to you, so there is nothing to see or delete there.
All I have about a person is what Apple gives me if they test TROME with TestFlight, and the messages they write to me.
I keep a message for as long as it takes to answer you and fix the problem, then I delete it, and sooner if you ask.
I keep the name and email address of a tester for the length of the test, then I remove them from my list of testers.

You can ask me to show you this data, to delete it, to correct it, or to limit how I use it, and you can object to my using it.
I answer within one month at most.

To ask a question, or to make one of these requests, write to <Contact />.
If you were invited to TestFlight by email and you want me to delete your data or to remove you from the testers, write from the address of the invitation: it is what lets me find your name.
An issue on GitHub with [the question form](https://github.com/valfur03/trome/issues/new?template=question.yml) works too for a question, but an issue is public and GitHub keeps it under its own [privacy policy](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement): do not write your name, your email address, or anything personal in it.
You can also complain to the [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en), the French data protection authority, or to the one of your own country.
